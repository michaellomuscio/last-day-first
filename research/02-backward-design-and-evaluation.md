## 1. Understanding by Design (UbD)

### 1.1 The framework, in my words

Primary sources used: McTighe & Wiggins, *Understanding by Design Framework* (ASCD white paper, (c) 2014): https://jaymctighe.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/UbD-White-Paper.pdf ; Wiggins, "UbD in a Nutshell" (c) 2006: https://www.jedc.org/stemak/sites/default/files/ubdnutshell.pdf

**SOURCE SAYS.** Backward design plans in three stages, and the authors stress that all three must align with each other and with standards: what Stage 1 names must be what Stage 2 assesses and Stage 3 teaches.

| Stage | Guiding questions (paraphrased) | What belongs here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Desired Results | What should students know, understand and be able to do? What transfer do we ultimately want? Which understandings and essential questions? | Established goals (standards etc.); transfer goals; enduring understandings; essential questions; knowledge and skill objectives |
| 2. Assessment Evidence | What will count as proof of understanding and transfer? How will performance be judged fairly? | Performance tasks (new, authentic situations) plus "other evidence" (quizzes, tests, observations, work samples); criteria/rubrics |
| 3. Learning Plan | How will learners come to understand and prepare to transfer? What sequence, activities, resources? | Learning events, each coded for which of the three goal types it serves; WHERETO review |

Key terms (white paper):
- **Established goals**: standards and curriculum expectations considered at the start of Stage 1.
- **Transfer goals**: long-term performance aims, what students should eventually do on their own with what they learned. The paper calls transfer the "bottom-line goal of education". Sample transfer goals in the paper are verbs of use ("apply mathematical knowledge... to solve real-world problems"; "critically appraise historical claims").
- **Enduring understandings**: written as full-sentence generalizations (the paper's samples: "Great literature explores universal themes of human existence...").
- **Essential questions**: open questions meant to be revisited over time, not answered in a single class period.
- **Knowledge and skills**: acquisition objectives targeted by standards. Treated as a means to meaning-making and transfer.
- **A-M-T**: Stage 3 plans for three goal types, acquisition, meaning-making, transfer. The authors suggest coding each learning event with the letter(s) A, M, T so that all three are addressed. They say teaching often stops at acquisition.
- **Twin sins**: "activity-oriented" planning (activities without clear priorities or purposes) and "coverage-oriented" planning (marching through a textbook). Backward design is said to help avoid both. (The paper uses the phrase "twin sins".)
- **Six facets of understanding**: explain, interpret, apply, perspective, empathy, self-knowledge. White paper FAQ: the facets are equal, not a hierarchy (unlike Bloom), and a designer picks only the facet(s) that fit the targeted understanding. Performance tasks built on them are culminating, not daily.
- **GRASPS** (Nutshell handout): a prompt for building authentic task scenarios. Letters: Goal, Role, Audience, Situation, Product/Performance, Standards (for success). The Nutshell handout confirms that GRASPS is the format for performance tasks; the individual letter expansions are the commonly published ones (SECONDARY for exact wording, search summary).
- **WHERETO** (Nutshell handout, Stage 3): Where is the unit headed / why; Hook; Equip and experience; Rethink and revise; Evaluate; Tailor; Organize. I confirmed "Where", "Hook", "Equip" and "Organize" verbatim in the handout; "Rethink", "Evaluate", "Tailor" from search summaries and the handout's "rethinking and reflection prompts" mention. Not an order of steps.
- The authors recommend unit-level, not isolated lesson-level, design because understandings, essential questions and transfer tasks are too big for a single lesson.
- They also say a textbook is a guide, not the curriculum.

### 1.2 Published review criteria: UbD Unit Design Standards 2.0

Source: https://jaymctighe.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/UbD-Design-Standards-2.0-Two-page-layout.pdf (I extracted text locally). Each criterion is rated Keep Working / Getting There / Meets Standard, with a feedback column. Paraphrased:

**Stage 1**
1. Understandings reflect conceptual "big ideas".
2. Understandings combine two or more concepts linked by connecting verbs.
3. Understandings are full-sentence generalizations.
4. Essential questions are open-ended (no single correct answer).
5. EQs require extended thinking and justification.
6. EQs stimulate and guide inquiry.
7. EQs can and should be revisited over time.
8. EQs clearly align with the identified understandings.
9. Knowledge and skills target established goals (e.g. standards).
10. Knowledge and skills will be explicitly taught and assessed in this unit.

**Stage 2**
1. Performance tasks give evidence of understanding and transfer.
2. Tasks require extended thinking and explanation, at DOK Level 3 or 4, "not just an answer".
3. Tasks set an authentic context with purpose, audience and real constraints (GRASPS).
4. Criteria/rubrics target distinct traits of understanding and performance.
5. Supplementary assessments give evidence of all other Stage 1 outcomes.

**Stage 3**
6. Learning events help learners acquire the Stage 1 knowledge/skills.
7. ...make meaning of important ideas.
8. ...transfer learning to new situations.
9. ...reflect the WHERETO elements.

**Overall**
10. All three stages are coherent and aligned.

(The numbering in the source restarts oddly on page 2; I preserved its numbering by stage.)

Copyright note: the PDF text I extracted carries no copyright or licence line. Treat as (c) the authors/publisher until you ask. Paraphrased checks are safer than reproducing the rubric wording.

## 2. Evidence that UbD / backward design works

Be honest: the evidence is **thin, mostly low-quality or indirect, and mostly produced or curated by people who favour the framework.**

1. **The developers' own caveat.** McTighe & Seif, *UbD Research Base* (undated, NCLB-era): because UbD is not a program with a fixed scope and sequence, "it is impossible at this time to provide direct, causal evidence of its effect on student achievement". They instead argue its principles match cognitive psychology (*How People Learn*, Bransford, Brown & Cocking 2000/2002): transfer needs conceptual understanding; breadth-over-depth curricula are a poor way to build expertise; formative feedback matters. https://www.jaymctighe.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/UbD-Research-Base.pdf (text extracted locally).
2. **The 2025 compilation.** McTighe's current "Research Studies on UbD" page claims "robust evidence" of gains and lists: Uluçınar (2021) meta-synthesis of 12 qualitative studies; quasi-experimental science studies (Özyurt et al. 2021, Turkey; Aslam et al. 2024, Pakistan grade 5; Almasaeid 2017, Dubai grade 8); small action-research studies (Yurtseven & Altun 2017 EFL; a grade-10 chemistry study); higher-education case studies; and implementation studies showing variable fidelity and need for training. https://jaymctighe.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/UbD-Research-Studies.docx-11.25.25.pdf . **Caution:** the document's own footnote says the table was generated with Perplexity Pro on 2025-10-29, and it labels studies "experimental" that other lines call quasi-experimental. I did not open the individual studies, so their quality is **UNVERIFIED**. The pattern (single-site, short, researcher-delivered units, different outcome measures) is typical of weak causal evidence (INFERENCE).
3. **Independent syntheses.** My searches found no rigorous independent systematic review or meta-analysis of UbD effects on student achievement. A secondary site cited a "Bowen (2017)" systematic review showing moderate gains; I could not find it. **UNVERIFIED; do not cite.**
4. **What the evidence does support (weakly):** teachers report clearer outcomes, tighter alignment and better coherence (Tumlos-Castillo 2015; medical education reports; per the compilation), but implementation is hard: unclear goals, little training, and time are repeated obstacles (Uluçınar 2021; Joshi 2021; Johnson et al. 2017; per compilation).
5. **Related evidence that is contested.** Formative assessment: Black & Wiliam (1998) is the usual citation for large gains; critics (Kingston & Nash 2011, via a secondary source) found a much smaller average (about 0.2) and noted that the "0.4 to 0.7" range traces mostly to an older special-education meta-analysis. https://www.edweek.org/education/has-the-research-on-formative-assessment-been-oversold/2009/05 (SECONDARY). Homework: see section 8.

**Design consequence (INFERENCE).** Frame the tool as "applying a widely used, theory-grounded planning discipline", not "proven to raise scores". The alignment checks stand on measurement logic (Webb, Porter, Biggs) as much as on UbD.

## 3. Constructive alignment (Biggs)

- Biggs (1996), "Enhancing teaching through constructive alignment", *Higher Education* 32, 347-364. Link: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2FBF00138871.pdf (the fetch returned an HTML shell; I did not read the text). Bibliographic details and the following idea are SECONDARY.
- **Idea (SECONDARY):** Define intended learning outcomes first; students construct meaning through learning activities; design teaching/learning activities and assessment tasks so that they target the same outcome. A later Biggs paper (HERDSA Review, via search summary) calls the verb in the outcome the link: the same verb should be realised in the activity and the assessment.
- **How alignment is checked (INFERENCE from the idea, matches UbD Overall criterion 10):** for each intended outcome, find (a) at least one activity that requires the verb, and (b) at least one assessment task that requires the verb at the same or a higher level. A rule checker can do this with a verb/level tag on every outcome, activity and assessment.

## 4. Alignment methods used in K-12

### 4.1 Webb's alignment criteria and Depth of Knowledge (DOK)

Source: Michigan Assessment Consortium's reproduction of Webb's criteria, https://www.michiganassessmentconsortium.org/wp-content/uploads/Mod-7Webb-Alignment-Study-Criteria.doc (read). Primary: Webb's 1997 monograph and 2002 "Depth-of-Knowledge Levels for Four Content Areas" (cited by secondary sources; not read; **UNVERIFIED titles from memory/secondary**).

**Four criteria (thresholds as stated in the source):**
1. **Categorical concurrence**: standards and assessment address the same content categories; acceptable when at least six items measure a standard.
2. **DOK consistency**: at least 50% of the items for an objective are at or above that objective's DOK level; 40-50% counts as "weakly" met.
3. **Range of knowledge correspondence**: at least 50% of a standard's objectives have at least one related item; 40-50% "weakly".
4. **Balance of representation**: an index where 1 is perfect balance; 0.7 or higher is acceptable; 0.6-0.7 "weakly".

These were built for state tests with many items. Applying them to a classroom unit is my adaptation (INFERENCE).

**DOK levels (names and definitions as given in the source):**
- **Level 1, Recall (and Reproduction)**: recall of facts, definitions, terms, simple procedures; one-step algorithms. Verbs: identify, recall, recognize, use, measure.
- **Level 2, Skill/Concept (Skills and Concepts)**: mental processing beyond a habitual response; deciding how to approach a problem. Verbs: classify, organize, estimate, compare data.
- **Level 3, Strategic Thinking**: reasoning, planning and using evidence; explaining thinking, making conjectures, justifying.
- **Level 4, Extended Thinking**: complex reasoning and planning, often over an extended time; multiple connections; e.g. designing experiments, developing and proving conjectures.

Level labels vary slightly across publishers (e.g. "Short-term Strategic Thinking" for Level 3), per secondary sources. DOK is about the complexity of thinking a task demands, not a hierarchy of difficulty and not a verb list; secondary sources stress this.

### 4.2 Porter's Surveys of Enacted Curriculum (SEC)

Source: Brookings paper on the SEC method, https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/1028_race_to_the_top_porter_paper.pdf (read).
- Content is a two-dimensional matrix: **topics** (hundreds of fine-grained topics grouped into general areas; 217 math and 163 ELA topics in the version described) by **five cognitive demand levels** that differ by subject.
- Analysts place each objective or test item into cells; proportions are averaged across analysts.
- **Alignment index = 1 - (sum over cells |x_i - y_i|) / 2**, where x and y are cell proportions in two documents; ranges 0 to 1, 1 = identical emphasis.
- Math cognitive demands named in the paper: memorize; perform procedures; demonstrate understanding; conjecture, generalize or prove; solve non-routine problems. (ELA levels: not verified.)
- Strengths per the paper: can collapse to general topics, can show under-tested, over-tested or wrong-level cells.
- Critique noted by secondary search results: questions about validity of the index for tests, and that higher-level cognitive objectives may be underweighted. (SECONDARY)

### 4.3 Hess Cognitive Rigor Matrix

Hess, Jones, Carlock & Walkup (2009), "Cognitive Rigor: Blending the Strengths of Bloom's Taxonomy and Webb's Depth of Knowledge to Enhance Classroom-Level Processes", ERIC ED517804; repository record https://shareok.org/handle/11244/336611 . Structure (SECONDARY from abstract and listings): a grid pairing Bloom's revised cognitive process levels (rows) with the four DOK levels (columns), with example tasks in the cells; subject versions exist (Reading/Writing; Math/Science). I did not read cell contents. Copyright: (c) Hess 2009; reproduction with full citation (SECONDARY). The Design Standards 2.0 doc above ties "extended thinking" to DOK 3-4.

### 4.4 Revised Bloom's taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001)

Primary: Anderson & Krathwohl (eds.), *A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing* (2001) (not read). Description is SECONDARY (university teaching centres; e.g. https://www.bu.edu/provost/files/2013/10/Anderson-Krathwohl-Revision-to-Blooms-Taxonomy-of-Educational-Objectives.pdf ).
- **Cognitive process dimension (six, as verbs):** Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create (Create now on top). 19 sub-processes are defined. From my recall of the book (UNVERIFIED in this pass): Remember = recognizing, recalling; Understand = interpreting, exemplifying, classifying, summarizing, inferring, comparing, explaining; Apply = executing, implementing; Analyze = differentiating, organizing, attributing; Evaluate = checking, critiquing; Create = generating, planning, producing (2+7+2+3+2+3 = 19, consistent with the "19 processes" claim).
- **Knowledge dimension (four, concrete to abstract):** Factual, Conceptual, Procedural, Metacognitive.
- An objective = verb (process) + noun (knowledge type), giving a cell in a 6 x 4 table.
- Wiggins & McTighe's FAQ in the white paper note Bloom is a hierarchy for item complexity while the six facets are not.

### 4.5 How the four methods relate (INFERENCE)

Webb and Hess/DOK measure the complexity of the task; Bloom measures the process plus knowledge type of the objective; SEC measures emphasis across topics x demand. For a no-AI tool, a teacher tags each goal and each assessment item with (topic/standard, Bloom process, DOK) and the tool compares proportions. Teachers will confuse Bloom and DOK, so the tool should offer both with verb-stem hints and require DOK to be chosen from the task description, not from the verb.

## 5. Assessment design options

### 5.1 Target-method match (Stiggins / Chappuis)

Source read: Stiggins, "The Quest for Quality", *Educational Leadership* (ASCD, 2005?): https://ascd.org/el/articles/the-quest-for-quality (year and issue not shown in the fetch; confirm). Also the Corwin/ATI chart (figure 3.9) at https://resources.corwin.com/sites/default/files/activity_3.4_with_figure_3.9.pdf (fetch failed; not read). The later Stiggins/Chappuis textbooks use five target types including **disposition**; that list is SECONDARY and I could not see the chart. The 2005 article gives four.

**Target types (article):** Knowledge; Reasoning; Performance Skill; Product. (Disposition: secondary, unverified.)
**Methods (article):** Selected response; Extended written response; Performance assessment; Personal communication.

Match as reported (article; my paraphrase):

| | Knowledge | Reasoning | Skill | Product |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selected response | Good | Good only for some reasoning patterns, out of context | Not good | Not good |
| Extended written response | Good for relationships among knowledge elements | Written solutions can reveal reasoning | Not addressed in the article | Strong only when the product is written |
| Performance assessment | Not good (too time-consuming to cover everything) | Good (observe problem solving) | Good | Good |
| Personal communication | Usable but time-consuming | Usable (think-alouds, follow-ups) | Strong only for oral communication skills | Not good |

Also in the article: "five keys to assessment quality": clear purpose; clear targets; sound design (match method to target, limit bias); effective communication of results; student involvement. The tool can ask "purpose" (who uses the result; formative or summative) as the first dropdown.

### 5.2 Test blueprints / tables of specifications

- A table of specifications (TOS) lists content areas against cognitive levels and the number or weight of items in each cell. Fives & DiDonato-Barnes (2013), *Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation*: a TOS helps teachers frame test construction and improve validity of classroom test-based evaluations. https://openpublishing.library.umass.edu/pare/article/id/1430 (SECONDARY via search snippet). Barnes, Fives & Krause (2014, *Assessment in Education*) found students using a TOS built tests with stronger content evidence but not stronger response-process evidence (SECONDARY).
- Notar et al. (2004) is often cited for TOS; I could not verify the citation. **UNVERIFIED.**
- Dropdown-ready blueprint fields (INFERENCE): content/standard tag x Bloom or DOK level x item format x points.

### 5.3 Formative vs summative; assessment purposes

- Stage 2 guidance and EQuIP (below) both want an assessment system with pre-, formative, summative and self-assessment components (EQuIP Science III.E: "Includes pre-, formative, summative, ..."; read).
- Options (INFERENCE from the sources): Purpose = diagnostic/pre; formative (feedback, no or low grading); summative (grade); self/peer assessment.
- Grading of formative work is contested (section 8).

### 5.4 Rubric types

- **Analytic**: separate criteria scored separately. **Holistic**: one overall score from a described level. (Definitions from a higher-education rubric article via search; SECONDARY.) Brookhart's rubric book is the standard teacher-level source but I did not retrieve it.
- **Single-point**: describes only the proficient level, with space for notes on where the work falls short or exceeds it; Fluckiger (2010), *Delta Kappa Gamma Bulletin* 76(4), 18-25; presented as primarily formative/developmental, not summative (SECONDARY). https://digitalcommons.unomaha.edu/tedfacpub/5
- Design standard link: UbD Stage 2 criterion 4 says rubrics should target distinct traits; EQuIP asks for aligned rubrics or scoring guidance.

### 5.5 Validity and reliability in classroom terms (INFERENCE; standard measurement ideas, not from a single fetched source)

- **Validity** = does the evidence support the decision I want to make about this student? Classroom proxies: content coverage (does the assessment sample the stated targets in the stated proportions), cognitive match (does it demand the intended level), absence of irrelevant barriers (reading level on a math test).
- **Reliability** = would a different day, scorer or item sample give about the same result? Classroom proxies: enough items per target, clear scoring criteria, scoring without knowing the student, second-scorer check on a sample.
- Wiggins argues authentic tasks get validity and reliability from standardizing tasks, criteria and standards (see next).

### 5.6 Wiggins on authentic assessment

Wiggins, "The Case for Authentic Assessment" (*Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation* 2(2), 1990), https://openpublishing.library.umass.edu/pare/article/id/1292 (SECONDARY, snippets only). Gist: assessment is authentic when we directly examine performance on worthy intellectual tasks; traditional tests depend on secrecy while real challenges are known in advance; validity and reliability come from standardizing appropriate tasks, criteria and standards. I did not retrieve the full criteria list; the UbD Design Standards ask for purpose, audience, and genuine constraints (GRASPS), which is the practical version.

### 5.7 Option lists for dropdowns (SYNTHESIS)

- Target type: knowledge / reasoning / skill / product (+ disposition, unverified).
- Assessment method: selected response / extended written response / performance assessment / personal communication. Optionally split selected response into MC, T/F, matching; and written response into short vs extended (the article only says "extended").
- Purpose: diagnostic / formative / summative / self-peer.
- Cognitive level: Bloom process (6), Bloom knowledge type (4), DOK (4), SEC math demand (5; optional).
- Understanding facet: explain / interpret / apply / perspective / empathy / self-knowledge.
- Rubric: analytic / holistic / single-point / none.
- Stage-1 element type: established goal / transfer goal / enduring understanding / essential question / knowledge objective / skill objective.
- Learning event goal type: A / M / T (multi-select).

## 6. Curriculum evaluation rubrics (criteria headings)

**EQuIP Rubric for Lessons and Units, ELA/Literacy 3-12** (Achieve; CC BY 3.0; version 06-24-13; read from PDF https://achievethecore.org/peersandpedagogy/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/EQuIP-ELArubric-06-24-13-FINAL-3-12.pdf ):
I. Alignment to the Depth of the CCSS (targets grade-level standards; clear purpose; text quality and complexity; for units, integrates reading, writing, speaking and listening).
II. Key Shifts in the CCSS (reading text closely; text-based evidence; writing from sources; academic vocabulary; for units, increasing text complexity, building knowledge, balance of texts, balance of writing).
III. Instructional Supports (engagement; scaffolds for varied learners; extensions; progression and gradual release; authentic application).
IV. Assessment (direct observable evidence of standards; unbiased and accessible methods; aligned rubrics or guidelines; varied modes including pre, formative, summative, self).
Scoring 0-3 per dimension; Dimension I is non-negotiable (a 0 or 1 ends the review).

**EQuIP Rubric for Science v3** (NextGenScience; CC BY 3.0; read): I. NGSS 3D Design (A. Explaining phenomena/designing solutions; B. Three dimensions; C. Integrating the three dimensions; unit-only: D. Unit coherence; E. Multiple science domains; F. Math and ELA). II. NGSS Instructional Supports (A. Relevance and authenticity; B. Student ideas; C. Building progressions; D. Scientific accuracy; E. Differentiated instruction; F. Teacher support for unit coherence; G. Scaffolded differentiation over time). III. Monitoring NGSS Student Progress (A. Monitoring 3D student performances; B. Formative; C. Scoring guidance; D. Unbiased tasks/items; E. Coherent assessment system; F. Opportunity to learn). (D under II headed "Scientific accuracy" is from my memory of the rubric; the extraction showed the other letters; **minor UNVERIFIED**.)
https://www.nextgenscience.org/sites/default/files/EQuIP%20Rubric%20for%20Science%20v3.pdf . Newer v3.1 exists; NGSS Lesson Screener is a shorter tool (SECONDARY). **EQuIP Math** exists as a separate K-12 rubric (SECONDARY; headings not verified; ELA and the general description both use four dimensions).

**EdReports** (https://cdn.edreports.org/reviewtools/math/2.0/Math-Grades-K-8-Draft-of-v2.0-Tools_-Review-Criteria.pdf , a "Draft of v2.0" tool, read): sequential gateways. Must meet or partially meet G1 to proceed; must meet G1 and G2 to be rated on G3. ELA gateways (SECONDARY): 1 Text Quality and Complexity; 2 Building Knowledge: Texts, Vocabulary, Tasks; 3 Usability. Publishers have challenged the gateway approach (SECONDARY, EdWeek).

**IMET** (Achieve / Student Achievement Partners; versions for K-8 math, ELA K-2 and 3-12, Aug 2015): Step structure with non-negotiable alignment criteria first, then indicators of quality; built for comprehensive programs (SECONDARY). Oregon has an NGSS-based OR-IMET for science 6-8 organised around focus, rigor and coherence (SECONDARY). **I could not open the IMET documents; criteria headings are UNVERIFIED.** Start from https://achievethecore.org (IMET page) before encoding.

**Other subjects (INFERENCE / gap):** I did not find a comparable rubric for social studies, world languages or arts in this pass. State instructional-materials reviews (e.g. Oregon, Kentucky) adapt IMET/EdReports patterns. A tool for non-ELA/math/science subjects will need its own checklist built from the common pattern: (1) alignment to standards (gatekeeper), (2) depth/rigor, (3) coherence across units, (4) supports for teachers and varied learners, (5) assessment quality and scoring guidance.

**Common structure across tools (INFERENCE):** a non-negotiable alignment gate; then quality dimensions; scoring 0-3 or Meets/Partially/Does not meet; evidence-citing. A tool can mimic that: a "Curated materials" module where the teacher answers yes/no/partly to a short list per gate, with a gate that blocks "approved" status if alignment is not met.

## 7. Scope, sequence, pacing

- **Curriculum mapping (Heidi Hayes Jacobs, *Mapping the Big Picture*, 1997; Hale & Jacobs, *A Guide to Curriculum Mapping*, 2008).** SECONDARY: map types are diary (what was taught when), projected, consensus (school-wide agreement by grade/subject) and essential (district/program-level). Typical map fields: unit, content, skills, assessments, resources, standards; essential and supporting questions appear as additional fields. https://www.sagepub.com/books/Book230412 , https://outreach.ou.edu/Educational-Professional-Development/PreK-12-Education/EDUTAS/Resources/Knowledgebases/Curriculum-Mapping-KnowledgeBases/Create-Diary-Maps
- **Prioritising standards (Ainsworth).** SECONDARY: criteria in Ainsworth's EdWeek post: **endurance** (lasts beyond one grade or course, needed in life); **leverage** (crossover use within and across content areas); **readiness** (needed for the next level; the example given is a skill tested on ACT/PSAT). Some districts add "assessed" (the R.E.A.L. acronym). Ainsworth says prioritising is about focus, not lowering the bar. https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-priority-standards-the-power-of-focus/2015/02 . I could not read the books (*Power Standards* 2004; *Prioritizing the Common Core* 2013).
- **Spiral curriculum.** Bruner, *The Process of Education* (1960): ideas revisited at increasing depth. Cited from memory; **UNVERIFIED in this pass.** UbD's white paper echoes the idea: essential questions are revisited over time; EQuIP ELA asks for a progression where concepts "advance and deepen over time".
- **Instructional days.** Pew (citing ECS, 2023): 27 of 38 states with a day requirement, plus DC, require 180 days; 7 require fewer (Colorado lowest at 160); 4 require more (Kansas leads). https://pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/09/07/in-the-u-s-180-days-of-school-is-most-common-but-length-of-school-day-varies-by-state . Many states set hours, not days. Older NCES table (2007-08 SASS): 180 days, about 6.6 hours per day nationally. https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/sass/tables/sass0708_035_s1s.asp
- **Days lost.** I found no authoritative national figure for days lost to testing, assemblies, absence. Kraft and colleagues' Providence study (SECONDARY, through a vendor blog summary) estimates interruptions cost roughly 10-20 days and about 16% of allocated elementary time. https://scale.stanford.edu/publications/understanding-disruptions-causes-and-variation-lost-instructional-time . The Berliner/Fisher "allocated vs engaged vs academic learning time" chain is also relevant. **Treat any default buffer as a teacher-adjustable parameter (INFERENCE).**
- **Block vs traditional.** Zepeda & Mayers (2006), *Review of Educational Research* 76(1), 137-170, reviewed 58 empirical studies; abstracts say grades/GPA and school climate tended to improve, while test scores and attendance were inconsistent and many studies were short or lacked detail (SECONDARY). https://www.proquest.com/docview/214116705 . I have no source for typical block vs traditional pacing arithmetic. INFERENCE for the tool: ask for period length and meeting pattern (daily / alternating / semester block) and compute minutes, not "days"; do not claim one schedule is better.

## 8. Syllabus content and grading policy

### 8.1 Syllabus
- Parkes & Harris (2002), "The Purposes of a Syllabus", *College Teaching* 50(2), 55-61: three roles: a **contract**, a **permanent record**, a **learning tool**; each role implies different content. https://wikicap.ulb.be/images/2/25/Parkes_Harris_2002.pdf (SECONDARY via search; not opened). Higher-education source, so transfer to high school is INFERENCE.
- Eberly, Newton & Wiggins (2001), *Journal of General Education* 50(1) 56-74, "The syllabus as a tool for student-centered learning": citation confirmed; content not seen.
- AP Course Audit pages require a course syllabus with evidence for each curricular requirement; the College Board publishes per-course Syllabus Development Guides and sample syllabi. https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-seminar/course-audit (SECONDARY; no list of required elements retrieved).
- **Component list (SYNTHESIS, not a verified standard):** course description and goals; unit overview and pacing; assessment types and weights; grading scale and category weights; late/missing work and retake policies; homework purpose and expected time; materials; attendance/communication; academic integrity; support and accommodations; contact and schedule. The contract/record/tool roles justify grouping them as: expectations (contract), content (record), learning supports (tool).

### 8.2 Grading policy: what research supports and where experts disagree

- **Research base.** Brookhart et al. (2016), "A Century of Grading Research", *Review of Educational Research* 86(4), 803-848, https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316672069 (SECONDARY): grades measure a multidimensional mix of achievement and non-achievement factors; early studies found teacher grades unreliable but teachers often had no clear criteria; unclear grading criteria produce misleading grades; authors suggest studying grading within instruction and assessment. Guskey's reading (SECONDARY): start from clear learning goals; use grades for feedback; limit the number of grade categories; no single "best practice".
- **Zeros / minimum grades.** Reeves (2004), "The Case Against the Zero", *Phi Delta Kappan* 86(4), 324-325, argues zero on a 100-point scale distorts averages (D-F gap is 60 points) and proposes a 50 floor. Critics say floors give unearned credit and lower accuracy; Texas legislated against requiring minimum grades. https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/the-unwinnable-battle-over-minimum-grades (SECONDARY). **Experts disagree.**
- **Homework.** Cooper, Robinson & Patall (2006), *Review of Educational Research* 76(1), 1-62: generally positive homework-achievement associations; stronger for grades 7-12 than K-6 (SECONDARY summaries; effect sizes quoted in summaries are unverified). Guskey lists including formative work and homework compliance in the grade average as "indefensible" (SECONDARY handout); equitable-grading advocates (Feldman) go further and propose no homework in the grade; a Fordham/RAND teacher survey reported most teachers disliked such practices (SECONDARY, The 74). **Disagreement is live.** A study of 779 urban high-school students found homework and employability points changed grades differently by socioeconomic status (SECONDARY).
- **Retakes / late work.** I found no strong K-12 causal study; one dissertation (Walden, 1,197 geometry students) found retake timing did not change growth (not peer reviewed). Retesting research outside K-12 is mixed (SECONDARY). Late-work penalty research: none found. **Say plainly that this is policy judgment backed mostly by argument, not experiment.**
- **Category weights.** No direct research found on the best weights. Guskey cites differing teacher gradebook categories (SECONDARY).
- **Tool implication (INFERENCE).** For each of these four choices, present options with a short "what the arguments are" note and a consistency check; do not mark any option as "wrong".

## 9. Proposed rule set (SYNTHESIS: my design, with source for each check)

Data model assumed: every Stage 1 element, assessment item/task, learning event and homework is a record with tags: type, linked records, Bloom process, Bloom knowledge type, DOK, A/M/T, facet(s), standard IDs, minutes/days, points/weight. Severity: E = error, W = warning, A = advisory. Thresholds in square brackets are tunable defaults.

### Stage 1 integrity
1. **IF** a unit has no element of type transfer goal **THEN** (W) "Name what students should eventually do on their own with this learning." *Source: UbD white paper Stage 1 question on ultimate transfer; Design Standards Stage 2 #1.*
2. **IF** an enduring understanding has no linked assessment item or task **THEN** (E) "Nothing will show whether students got this." *Source: UbD alignment ("are the assessments aligned to all Stage 1 elements?"); Design Standards Overall #10.*
3. **IF** an enduring understanding is not tagged as a sentence relating two or more named concepts (picker: concept A + connecting verb + concept B) **THEN** (W) "This reads like a topic or a fact; state a generalisation." *Source: Design Standards Stage 1 #1-3.*
4. **IF** an essential question is tagged "has a single correct answer" or has no linked enduring understanding **THEN** (W) revise or link. *Source: Design Standards Stage 1 #4, #5, #8.*
5. **IF** an essential question appears in only one lesson **THEN** (A) "Essential questions are meant to be revisited." *Source: Design Standards Stage 1 #7; white paper FAQ 3.*
6. **IF** a knowledge/skill objective has no linked established goal, **or** is not both linked to at least one assessment item and at least one learning event **THEN** (E) *Source: Design Standards Stage 1 #9-10.*
7. **IF** the number of priority objectives in a unit exceeds [what the teaching days can support, e.g. more than 1 priority objective per 2 instructional days] **THEN** (W) "Too much to teach deeply; rank." *Source: white paper ("more content than can reasonably be addressed"); Ainsworth focus. The numeric ratio is INFERENCE.*

### Stage 2 and alignment
8. **IF** an assessed goal has DOK/Bloom tag higher than every assessment item linked to it **THEN** (E) "The assessment asks for less thinking than the goal requires." *Source: Webb DOK consistency; Biggs verb alignment.*
9. **IF** fewer than 50% of items for a goal are at or above the goal's DOK **THEN** (W; 40-50% = weakly met). *Source: Webb criterion 2 (thresholds verified).*
10. **IF** fewer than 50% of a standard's objectives have any linked item **THEN** (W) coverage gap; **IF** a priority objective has none **THEN** (E). *Source: Webb range-of-knowledge (50%/40-50%), Design Standards Stage 2 #5.*
11. **IF** a summative assessment's blueprint differs from the teaching-time/priority weights with alignment index [1 - sum|x-y|/2 < 0.7] **THEN** (W) "Emphasis does not match what you taught." *Source: Porter's SEC index (formula verified); 0.7 threshold borrowed from Webb balance (INFERENCE).*
12. **IF** a priority objective is assessed by fewer than [6] scored items or points-equivalent in a summative **THEN** (A) "Thin evidence; consider adding items or a second assessment". *Source: Webb categorical concurrence (six items, designed for large tests; classroom use is INFERENCE).*
13. **IF** a target type and assessment method pair is rated "not a good match" **THEN** (W) show the better methods. Examples: skill target with only selected response; knowledge target with only performance assessment; product target with selected response. *Source: Stiggins (2005).*
14. **IF** a unit has a transfer goal but no performance task, **or** the task's DOK is below 3 **THEN** (W). *Source: Design Standards Stage 2 #1-2.*
15. **IF** a performance task is missing any of Goal, Role, Audience, Situation, Product, Standards **THEN** (A) show the missing field. *Source: GRASPS (Nutshell; Design Standards Stage 2 #3).*
16. **IF** a major assessment has rubric = none, or an analytic rubric has fewer than 2 criteria, or criteria are not linked to Stage 1 goals **THEN** (W). *Source: Design Standards Stage 2 #4; EQuIP Science III.C; Stiggins keys (clear targets).*
17. **IF** a selected-response or short-answer summative has no blueprint/table of specifications **THEN** (A) offer to generate an empty table. *Source: TOS literature (Fives & DiDonato-Barnes 2013).*
18. **IF** a graded summative has no earlier formative or pre-assessment tagged to the same objectives **THEN** (W) "No chance to practise or adjust before the graded event". *Source: EQuIP Science III.E (coherent assessment system), Stiggins purpose key.*
19. **IF** a unit has no self-assessment or reflection item **THEN** (A). *Source: facet of self-knowledge; EQuIP ELA IV "self-assessment"; Stiggins student-involvement key.*

### Stage 3 and alignment
20. **IF** a unit has no learning events tagged M, or none tagged T, or none tagged A **THEN** (W) per missing type. **IF** more than [80%] of event time is A only **THEN** (W) "mostly delivery". *Source: white paper (A-M-T coding; teaching often stops at acquisition).*
21. **IF** a learning event is linked to no assessed objective **THEN** (W) "activity with no stated purpose" (activity-oriented sin).
22. **IF** a unit's lesson order is tagged "follows textbook" and the unit has no priority-ranked goals **THEN** (W) "coverage-driven plan". *Source: white paper twin sins and FAQ 7.* (21 and 22 together are the twin-sins checks.)
23. **IF** an assessed objective has no learning event that teaches it **THEN** (E) "You are assessing what you have not planned to teach." *Source: Design Standards Stage 1 #10, Stage 3 #6; Biggs alignment.*
24. **IF** fewer than 7 WHERETO boxes are ticked **THEN** (A) list unticked elements. *Source: Design Standards Stage 3 #9; Nutshell. Treat as prompts, not requirements: WHERETO is not a sequence.*
25. **IF** a lesson's planned minutes exceed the period length set in the schedule **THEN** (E). *Pure arithmetic; INFERENCE.*
26. **IF** homework has no linked objective, or its stated purpose is "none/compliance" **THEN** (W); **IF** estimated daily homework minutes across the teacher's courses exceed a school-set limit **THEN** (A). *Source: Cooper et al. (secondary) on homework value; inference.*

### Course level
27. **IF** the sum of unit days exceeds available days after the teacher-set buffer [default 10%], or is below [80%] of available days **THEN** (W) pacing over/under-committed. *Source: Pew 180 days; Kraft Providence loss estimates (secondary); buffer is INFERENCE.*
28. **IF** a standard in the course list maps to no unit **THEN** (E); **IF** a standard flagged priority (endurance, leverage, readiness all "yes") has fewer instructional days than the average non-priority standard **THEN** (W); **IF** more than [50%] of standards are flagged priority **THEN** (A) "priorities are not priorities". *Source: Ainsworth criteria (secondary); curriculum-mapping practice; thresholds INFERENCE.*
29. **IF** a priority skill/concept is tagged "recurring" but appears in only one unit, or a priority standard is assessed once across the year **THEN** (A) consider spiralling. *Source: Bruner spiral (unverified), UbD revisit idea, EQuIP progression.*
30. **IF** category weights do not sum to 100%, **or** a category exists in the syllabus that the gradebook plan lacks (or vice versa), **or** late-work policy, retake policy or homework weight is unset **THEN** (E/W) prompt; **IF** formative/practice work is weighted above [20%] **THEN** (A) show "experts disagree" panel (Guskey vs others). *Source: Brookhart et al. 2016 (clear criteria); Guskey (secondary); thresholds INFERENCE.*

### Curated materials (adopted/found materials)
31. **IF** a curated resource fails the "aligned to the unit's standards" gate **THEN** (E) block "approved" status; later criteria are only informational. *Source: EQuIP and EdReports gating logic; IMET non-negotiable step (secondary).* (31st check; drop if you want exactly 30.)
